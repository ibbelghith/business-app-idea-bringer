# Country market scan

Use this reference only for `discover` or when a changed country/region materially changes an existing evaluation. The selected country is the boundary for research; do not use a global trend as evidence of local demand.

## 1. Lock the market boundary

Record country, analysis date, region/city if relevant, target language(s), customer type, and founder constraints. Distinguish nationwide from urban or regional evidence. Note which languages and regions were not searched. If country is unknown in a new discovery session, ask for it before starting.

## 2. Research conditions that affect a software business

Research only factors that could change the reachable buyer, the workflow, willingness to pay, product delivery, competition, or downside:

- population, household/business counts, sector mix, and informality where useful;
- connectivity, device access, digital literacy, and infrastructure reliability;
- languages and local discovery/trust channels;
- payment methods, purchasing power, and common transaction patterns;
- business registration, taxes, logistics, import/export conditions where relevant;
- current regulations affecting the proposed customer, data, payments, employment, or sector;
- differences among regions, urban/rural markets, and customer sizes.

Use PESTLE as a prompt to avoid missing an external factor, not as a reason to fill a generic checklist. A factor with no plausible effect on a specific opportunity can be omitted.

## 3. Choose evidence sources for the claim

Prefer sources produced in or for the selected country:

1. National statistics office, central bank, regulator, official gazette/laws, ministries, public procurement, and official open data.
2. Local industry associations, transparent local surveys, academic work, and credible multilateral studies with a clear country sample.
3. Country-specific competitor pricing/features, app listings and reviews, directories, job listings, procurement, search signals, and public community discussions.
4. Direct customer interviews, workflow observation, records customers choose to share, and behavior-based tests.

Use global market reports only for global context unless the methodology supports a defensible country estimate. Treat online communities and app-store reviews as selected samples, not representative surveys. For law and regulation, use the current local authority text and record the effective date; do not infer compliance from secondary commentary.

## 4. Identify workflows, not fashionable sectors

For each signal, ask: which exact customer has the problem, what were they trying to get done, how is the work handled today, how costly/recurrent is it, who pays, and where can the first customers be reached? A large sector, subsidy, national problem, or government priority does not automatically create a sellable app. Narrow the domain to a customer group plus a repeatable job and plausible buyer.

For two-sided or operationally intensive concepts, identify each side of the transaction and the manual, logistics, support, compliance, and trust costs. An app may be only one part of the service.

## 5. Turn domains into app concepts

For each surviving domain, propose only concepts that could plausibly improve the existing workflow. Explicitly state what stays manual in the first test. Match product behavior to local conditions (for example, language, intermittent connectivity, cash alongside digital payments, low-end devices, or existing messaging tools) only when supported by local evidence.

Do not recommend an app simply because a workflow is not digitized. Manual processes may be cheap, trusted, legally required, or more flexible. Name the digital alternative and status quo, then test whether software creates enough value to justify switching.

## 6. Report coverage and gaps

Give each important market claim a source record and claim ID. Summarize coverage explicitly: geography, languages, customer segments, channels, and date range researched; major access barriers; and any missing source families. Failed searches, unavailable local-language sources, paywalls, or stale data remain unknowns, not negative findings.

## Country-first discovery output

The report's leading results are opportunity **domains** first and **app ideas** second. For each ranked domain/concept, show:

- country/region and customer group;
- job, pain, current alternative, and likely buyer;
- local evidence for and against, with stable claim/evidence IDs;
- confidence/coverage and material unknowns;
- first-customer route and any fatal gate;
- app concept and why software may help;
- the cheapest behavior-based test and decision thresholds.

A country report can conclude “no candidate yet.” Do not invent opportunities to fill the list.
